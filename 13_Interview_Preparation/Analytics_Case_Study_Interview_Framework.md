# Analytics Case Study Interview Framework

## Overview

Analytics case study interviews evaluate your ability to solve ambiguous business problems using data.

Unlike SQL interviews, case studies do not only test technical skills.

They evaluate:

- Business understanding
- Analytical thinking
- Problem-solving approach
- Metric design
- Communication
- Ability to translate insights into actions

A strong analytics engineer should demonstrate a structured approach from business problem to data solution.

---

# The Analytics Case Study Framework

Use this framework:

```
Understand Business Problem

↓

Define Success Metrics

↓

Identify Required Data

↓

Analyze Data

↓

Find Insights

↓

Recommend Actions

↓

Measure Impact
```

---

# Step 1: Understand The Business Problem

Before analyzing data, clarify the problem.

Ask:

- What decision are we trying to make?
- Who is the stakeholder?
- What outcome are we trying to improve?
- What does success look like?

---

# Example

Business Question:

"Sales have decreased. Why?"

Weak approach:

```
Immediately analyze revenue data.
```

Strong approach:

```
Understand which sales metric declined,
when it started, and which business areas
are affected.
```

---

# Step 2: Define Success Metrics

Translate business goals into measurable KPIs.

---

# Example: Customer Retention

Business Goal:

"Improve retention."

Metrics:

```
Customer Retention Rate

↓

Churn Rate

↓

Repeat Purchase Rate

↓

Customer Lifetime Value
```

---

# Example: Sales Growth

Metrics:

```
Revenue

↓

Average Order Value

↓

Number Of Orders

↓

Customer Count
```

---

# Step 3: Identify Required Data

Determine what data is needed.

---

# Example: E-commerce Sales Decline

Required data:

## Orders

```
order_id

customer_id

product_id

date

revenue
```

---

## Customers

```
customer_id

location

segment
```

---

## Products

```
product_id

category

price
```

---

# Step 4: Analyze Data

Use structured analysis.

---

# Descriptive Analysis

Question:

"What happened?"

Examples:

- Revenue trends
- Customer counts
- Product performance

---

# Diagnostic Analysis

Question:

"Why did it happen?"

Examples:

- Customer behavior
- Regional differences
- Product issues

---

# Predictive Analysis

Question:

"What may happen?"

Examples:

- Churn prediction
- Demand forecasting

---

# Prescriptive Analysis

Question:

"What should we do?"

Examples:

- Marketing actions
- Product improvements

---

# Step 5: Find Insights

A good insight contains:

```
Observation

+

Explanation

+

Business Impact
```

---

# Weak Insight

"Sales decreased."

---

# Strong Insight

"Revenue declined 25% in the last quarter, primarily driven by a 40% drop in repeat purchases among customers acquired through paid advertising."

---

# Step 6: Recommend Actions

Recommendations should be:

- Specific
- Data-driven
- Practical
- Measurable

---

# Example

Insight:

New users stop using the product after signup.

Recommendation:

Improve onboarding by introducing guided setup tutorials.

---

# Step 7: Measure Impact

Define how success will be tracked.

Example:

Action:

Improve onboarding.

Measurement:

```
Activation Rate

+

7-Day Retention

+

Feature Adoption
```

# Case Study Answer Structure

Use:

## 1. Clarify

"I would first understand the business objective and define the success metric."

---

## 2. Approach

"I would analyze trends, segment the data, and identify key drivers."

---

## 3. Findings

"The analysis would reveal the main contributors to the problem."

---

## 4. Recommendation

"I would recommend actions based on the identified drivers."

---

## 5. Measurement

"I would track KPIs to evaluate impact."

---

# Example Case Study

## Problem

A SaaS company has increasing customer churn.

---

# Step 1: Clarify

Questions:

- What period increased churn?
- Which customers are affected?
- How is churn defined?

---

# Step 2: Define Metrics

```
Monthly Churn Rate

Customer Lifetime Value

Active Users

Feature Adoption
```

---

# Step 3: Analyze

Investigate:

```
Customer Usage

↓

Subscription Plans

↓

Support Tickets

↓

Feature Engagement
```

---

# Step 4: Findings

Example:

"Customers who use fewer than two core features have 3x higher churn."

---

# Step 5: Recommendation

"Improve onboarding and encourage adoption of core features."

---

# Step 6: Measure

Track:

```
Feature Adoption

↓

Retention Rate

↓

Churn Reduction
```

Analytics engineers should also discuss:

## Data Modeling

Example:

"I would create customer activity models to track engagement."

---

## Data Quality

Example:

"I would validate event completeness before analyzing churn."

---

## Metrics Layer

Example:

"I would standardize churn definitions so teams use consistent metrics."

# Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1

Jumping into SQL immediately.

---

## Mistake 2

Not clarifying business goals.

---

## Mistake 3

Listing observations without recommendations.

---

## Mistake 4

Ignoring data limitations.

---

## Mistake 5

Creating metrics without business meaning.

---

# Case Study Checklist

Before answering:

```
✓ Understand Problem

✓ Identify Stakeholders

✓ Define Metrics

✓ Identify Data Sources

✓ Analyze Drivers

✓ Explain Insights

✓ Recommend Actions

✓ Measure Success
```

---

# Key Takeaway

Analytics case studies test whether you can move from:

```
Business Question

↓

Data Analysis

↓

Insight

↓

Decision
```

The strongest analytics engineers do not just produce numbers.

They create understanding that helps organizations make better decisions.
